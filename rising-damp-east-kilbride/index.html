Rising Damp Treatment in East Kilbride

Professional rising damp assessment and treatment arranged with local specialists.

Rising damp occurs when groundwater moves upward through the masonry of a building by capillary action. It is more common in older properties where the original damp proof course has deteriorated, been bridged, or was never present in the original construction.

Rising damp is also one of the most frequently misdiagnosed damp problems. A professional assessment is important before any treatment is agreed to confirm it is genuinely the cause.

Signs of Rising Damp

  • A distinctive tide mark or staining on internal walls, typically between 300mm and 1.5 metres from floor level
  • White salt deposits or crystalline patches on the surface of walls
  • Plaster that is soft, powdery, or crumbling at lower levels
  • Damp patches that do not dry out, even during warm and dry periods
  • Skirting boards that are rotting or deteriorating at their base
  • A damp, musty smell concentrated near floor level

These signs can also be caused by other moisture problems including condensation and penetrating damp. A specialist will take measurements and assess the pattern of moisture before confirming whether rising damp is the cause.

How Rising Damp Is Treated

1

Survey and Diagnosis

Moisture readings are taken and the pattern of damp is assessed to confirm rising damp is present and identify how far it has progressed.

2

Chemical DPC Injection

A water-repellent chemical is injected along the base of the affected wall to form a new damp proof barrier, preventing further moisture from rising.

3

Replastering

Affected plaster is removed and replaced using salt-resistant render once the treatment is complete and the wall has begun to dry out.

4

Review of Contributing Factors

Any external factors such as raised ground levels or bridged cavities that may cause moisture to bypass the new DPC are identified and addressed.

Rising Damp in East Kilbride

East Kilbride has a mix of post-war housing built across several decades. Properties from the 1950s through to the early 1980s may have original damp proof courses that are now showing signs of age or failure. Ground levels around some properties have also changed over the years through landscaping and alterations, which can compromise what was originally an effective DPC. Local specialists who work regularly in the area will often have a good understanding of which property types are most commonly affected.
