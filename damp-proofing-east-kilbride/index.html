Damp Proofing in East Kilbride

Long-term damp proofing solutions arranged with trusted local specialists.

Damp proofing refers to the range of treatments and systems used to prevent moisture from entering or rising through the walls and floors of a property. For many East Kilbride homeowners, damp proofing becomes relevant when an existing damp proof course has failed, when penetrating damp is identified following a survey, or where a property has never had adequate moisture protection.

When Damp Proofing May Be Needed

  • Visible damp patches on interior walls, particularly at lower levels
  • A failed or absent damp proof course identified during a survey
  • Water penetrating through external walls during or after rainfall
  • Damp problems in a basement or below-ground area
  • Recurring damp issues that have not been resolved by previous treatment
  • Salt deposits or tide marks on internal walls near floor level

Not every damp problem requires damp proofing. A professional assessment will confirm whether it is the appropriate solution for your specific situation before any treatment is recommended.

Types of Damp Proofing

Depending on the cause and location of the damp, a specialist may recommend one or more of the following approaches:

1

Chemical DPC Injection

A water-repellent chemical solution is injected into the mortar course of the wall to create a new barrier against rising damp. One of the most common treatments for failed or absent damp proof courses in older properties.

2

Waterproof Render and Tanking

Specialist waterproof coatings or systems applied to walls or floors. Most commonly used in basements and areas subject to water pressure from outside.

3

Cavity and Drainage Solutions

In cases involving penetrating damp, improvements to drainage, cavity walls, or external pointing may be recommended alongside or as an alternative to chemical treatment.

Damp Proofing in Older East Kilbride Properties

A number of homes in East Kilbride, particularly those built in the 1960s and 1970s, have original damp proof courses that have reached the end of their effective lifespan. In some cases, alterations made over the years, such as raised external ground levels or extensions, have bridged or compromised the existing DPC. Local specialists are familiar with these patterns and can often identify the likely issue during an initial assessment.
